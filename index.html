01 · The brief

An environment, not a backdrop

On Vision Pro, Max offers immersive environments: full 360° spaces that wrap around you while you browse and watch. After the House of the Dragon Throne Room, the next destination was Hogwarts, and there was only one room it could be.

The goal: a Great Hall that feels alive at true scale, runs comfortably on-device, and works in two moods: a candle-lit night and a bright day.

02 · Project setup · Travis

From game assets to a clean scene

Our starting point was video-game assets from Avalanche Software, the team behind Hogwarts Legacy. Game assets are built for a game engine, with shared trim sheets, LODs and real-time lighting. Vision Pro needed something leaner: a self-contained scene with lighting baked into every surface.

I set up the project: importing the source assets into Blender, establishing scale and orientation for Reality Composer Pro, and naming and organising every object so a three-person team could texture, bake and light in parallel without stepping on each other.

  • Consistent SM_HW_GH_* naming by asset family
  • _Baked suffix marking every bake target, one texture per object
  • VFX_* prefix for special-case materials like the rafters

    03 · Mesh clean-up & UVs · Travis

    Tables, UVs & seam sewing

    The four long house tables and their benches are the heart of the room, and they're seen up close. I cleaned up their geometry, then re-unwrapped the UVs so each table could receive its own high-resolution bake. The tables carry a dedicated fixUvMap UV set that the bake reads from.

    Baking lighting exposes every UV seam: a split island means a visible line in the shadow. I sewed seams back together wherever the eye travels, so the baked light flows across the tabletops and runners without breaks.

    The checker you see on the tables right now is a live UV visualisation on the real meshes; cyan marks the seams. In the shipped environment the house tables appear in day mode, so the hall switches to day for this chapter.

    04 · Enchanted Ceiling · Travis

    A sky that moves

    The ceiling is the signature of the room, so it couldn't be a static picture. I built it as an animated shader in Reality Composer Pro that composites several layers over a deep sky gradient: a star field, drifting cloud sheets moving at different speeds, and procedurally generated noise clouds.

    The same graph drives both moods: night blends stars and cool moonlit clouds, day swaps to daylight cloud layers. A soft pill-shaped opacity mask feathers the sky into the stonework so the edges never read as a flat plane.

    05 · Transparent rafters · Travis

    Rafters that dissolve into the sky

    The Great Hall has heavy timber rafters, but the Enchanted Ceiling needed to read as open sky. Making them transparent on Vision Pro required more attention because blended surfaces stacked in front of each other, creating a less than ideal result.

    My approach: render the rafters with an unlit material whose opacity comes from world-space height. The shader reads the object's position, takes its Y value and remaps it: fully solid at 15 m, gone by 30 m. Because the fade is driven by height, not by a texture, it stays seamless across every rafter.

    The final step: the rafters sit in their own "mesh sorting group" with a depth pre-pass. Depth is written first, so only the nearest rafter surface gets shaded at each pixel. The fade then behaves as if the rafters were a flat rendered image: overlapping faces don't stack up into extra opacity, and the timber dissolves evenly as it rises.

    06 · Fire · Mark

    A fire that never goes out

    Mark Brouwer did a lot of the texturing and created the flames. Real fire footage was converted into flipbook sprite sheets (an 8-second clip becomes a grid of frames on one texture), and a custom shader steps through the frames every tick.

    It's an efficient way to put high-quality looping motion into the environment: the great fireplace and the house torches are just flat cards with an animated texture, plus flickering dynamic lights.

    07 · Floating candles · Mark

    Hundreds of candles, none of them still

    Mark also built the floating candles. Their placement was scattered in Blender; their motion lives in Reality Composer Pro, where a dynamically generated noise field offsets each candle's position so the whole cloud bobs gently and never moves in lockstep.

    08 · Lighting & final renders · Luke

    Two moods, baked in

    Luke Lavictoire lit the hall and produced the final renders for both modes. Lighting was built in Blender and baked into the textures. Every surface you're looking at carries its light with it, which is how a scene this rich stays fast on-device.

    Night is warm firelight and candle glow under a starry sky; day is cool window light and soft clouds. Each mode is its own full set of baked textures on the same geometry.

    09 · In the headset

    Watch anything from the Great Hall

    In the app, the hall wraps around you and the video player docks in the middle of the room, so you can watch in the Great Hall itself. The team also shipped Browse & Watch modes and an environment downloader that delivers large environments as remote assets instead of bundling them with the app.
